# Vendor Performance — Insights Report

**Scope:** 2024 purchases and sales for 80 stores · 119 vendors · 8,564 profitable vendor-product lines (after dropping loss-making, unsold and zero-sales lines from 10,692).

## 1. Headline numbers
- Sales **$441.4M**, purchases **$307.3M**, gross profit **$134.1M**, overall margin **30.4%**.
- **$2.71M** of purchased stock remained unsold at year end.

## 2. Findings

### Supplier concentration
- Top 10 vendors account for **65.69%** of purchase spend; Diageo North America alone is **16.3%**.
- Risk: a supply problem or price rise at one of these vendors hits two-thirds of the business.

### Top sellers
- Vendors: Diageo ($67.99M), Martignetti ($39.33M), Pernod Ricard ($32.06M), Jim Beam ($31.42M), Bacardi ($24.85M).
- Brands: Jack Daniel's No 7 ($7.96M), Tito's ($7.40M), Grey Goose ($7.21M), Captain Morgan ($6.36M), Absolut ($6.24M).

### Bulk purchasing
- Order lines split into three equal groups by quantity: average unit cost **Small $39.07 → Medium $15.49 → Large $10.78** (~72% lower for large).
- Caveat: small-quantity lines also include premium/expensive products, so part of the gap is product mix, not only volume discounts.

### Slow-moving stock and locked capital
- 4,228 lines have stock turnover below 1 (bought more than sold).
- Lowest average turnover: Alisa Carr Beverages (0.62), Highland Wine Merchants (0.71), Park Street Imports (0.75).
- Most capital locked: Diageo $722K, Jim Beam $555K, Pernod Ricard $471K, William Grant $402K, E&J Gallo $228K.

### Pricing / promotion candidates
- **198 brands** are in the bottom 15% of sales (≤ $560) but top 15% of margin (≥ 65%) — e.g. Santa Rita Organic Sauvignon Blanc, Crown Royal Apple, Dad's Hat Rye.

### Margin vs sales volume (statistics)
- Top 25% sellers: mean margin **31.2%** (95% CI 30.7–31.6).
- Bottom 25% sellers: mean margin **41.6%** (95% CI 40.5–42.6).
- Welch's t-test: t = −17.67, p < 0.001 → the difference is statistically significant: high-volume products run on thinner margins.

## 3. Recommendations
1. **Reduce dependence** on the top 10 vendors — qualify alternative suppliers for key categories and use the volume to negotiate better terms.
2. **Promote the 198 high-margin, low-sales brands** (placement, bundles, targeted discounts) — each extra sale earns ~65%+ margin.
3. **Free up the $2.71M** in unsold stock: clearance pricing for slow movers, smaller and more frequent orders from low-turnover vendors.
4. **Use bulk buying selectively** — large orders cut unit cost, but only for products with turnover near or above 1.
5. **Protect top-seller margins** — review pricing and freight/logistics costs for high-volume lines, where margins are lowest.
